# M2 — the kart drives, and which end is the front

**Status: settled. The kart's authored long axis is local X, its nose is local −X,
and the derived yaw correction is +90°.**

This change gives the simulation a driver, the world a kart, and the game its
first input. The interesting part is none of those: it is establishing **which end
of the kart is the front**, because the check that looks like it settles that
does not.

## What could not be established by testing

The design document's §4 warns that getting this wrong "produces a kart that crabs
sideways, which is the classic failure of this asset". The natural test — drive
from 16 headings and check the kart moves the way it faces — **passes on a kart
driving backwards**. It derives the nose direction from `KART_YAW_CORRECTION`, the
same constant it is testing, so a sign error makes both sides wrong together and
all 32 heading/direction combinations agree.

Demonstrated, not assumed. With `NOSE_SIGN` inverted:

| Check | On a tail-first kart |
|---|---|
| 16-heading travel test | **passes** |
| Footprint conformance | **passes** — a half-turn does not transpose an AABB |
| Vertex-centroid corroboration | fails |
| Derived correction vs the document's stated +90° | fails |

## What did establish it

Three witnesses, none of which is the travel test.

1. **The capture.** `2026-08-29-m2-kart-front.png`, from the +Z side looking back at
   the origin. The steering wheel is visible in front of the seat and the floor pan
   extends toward the camera, so we are looking at the kart's front while the kart
   faces world forward. The first attempt at this rendered the kart tail-first and
   the capture is what caught it.
2. **The mesh's own asymmetry.** The vertex centroid sits **+0.0764** from the
   bounding-box centre along the long axis — toward the engine, seat and rear
   tyres. The nose is the end the mass is not at, which puts it at local −X, with
   no reference to the capture. `tests/kart_test.gd` checks this, and refuses to
   speak if a future mesh is too symmetric for the heuristic to mean anything.
3. **The document's own figure.** §4 states a +90° correction. It was **not**
   transcribed — A6 records why it could not be, being stated in the reference
   build's frame — so the port derives its own from the imported bounds and gets
   **+90°**. The two agreeing is a fact worth pinning;
   `tools/check_kart_conformance.py` fails if they ever diverge.

## The conformance check reads the design document

Every other numeric gate here bottoms out in `data/tuning.json`, which is our
transcription. `check_kart_conformance.py` reads §1's authored box, §3's final
dimensions and world footprint, and §4's correction **from the document**, and
compares them against what Godot built — so the two sides share no ancestor.

| | measured | the document |
|---|---|---|
| Authored box | 2.000 × 1.019 × 1.868 | §1 inventory |
| Scale | ×1.17814 | 1.20 / 1.019 |
| World extent at the start line | 2.20 on X, 2.36 on Z | §3, in those words |
| Yaw correction | +90° | §4 |

Verified by editing the design document and watching the gate fail — the oracle
really is the document, not a copy of it.

## The contact shadow, and a measurement that was wrong

The design document calls the kart's contact shadow "the primary cue for where the
kart actually is on the ground", and A7 and A8 were settled to protect it — on the
renderer spike's scene, not this one.

**It survives here.** `2026-08-29-m2-kart-contact-shadow.png`, measured by
`tools/measure_contact_shadow.py`: shadow depth **50.3%** against a floor of 21.6%,
where fully shadowed ground would read 43.3% darker.

**A first pass at this concluded there was no shadow at all**, from a −3%
measurement. That was wrong, and the way it was wrong is worth keeping: it took the
**median of a hand-picked box** beneath the kart. At a sun elevation of 49.7° the
shadow is offset barely 1 wu from a kart 2.2 wu wide, so it falls largely under the
kart's own footprint, and any box big enough to pick by hand is mostly lit ground.
`measure_shadow.py`'s own comments record the spike learning this in its first run.
This is the second time the project has paid for it, which is why the rule —
darkest decile, named region, never a hand-picked median — now appears in two
tools.

**One thing worth knowing for M6.** A8's decision still helps here but far less than
it did on the spike's scene. Shadowed ground near the kart is 10.2% with the
normal bias at 0.1 against 8.0% at Godot's 2.0 — a two-point gap, where the spike
measured 27.2% against 6.5%. The decision stands; the margin that justified it does
not reproduce at this framing, and that is a candidate for M6 to revisit rather
than something to fix here.

## What these captures do not show

No props, no collision, no lap timing, no HUD, no chase camera. The viewpoint is
still the placeholder `add-world-presentation-layer` shipped, and its field of view
is Godot's default 75 — which happens to equal the document's `fovBase`, a
coincidence and not a decision. `add-chase-camera` replaces it.
