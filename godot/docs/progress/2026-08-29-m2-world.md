# M2 — the world, and settling the exposure (A9)

**Status: settled. The light scale is 0.2809 — see the Verdict.**

The first change of M2 builds the design document's §5 environment and §6
lighting, and has to settle **A9**: the document states its light intensities in
the reference build's units, and at face value under Godot they clip most of the
frame.

## What is being decided

The document's **ratios** are normative. The absolute scale cannot be — the
document is engine-agnostic and could not state a Godot value if it wanted to. So
the port chooses one number, and the question is how to choose it defensibly.

## The criteria, fixed before any image was judged

1. **C1** — no saturated pixel **anywhere in the frame**, in any channel. The
   scene is ground, sky, an unlit grid and an unlit band, none of which carries a
   specular highlight, so a saturated pixel is exposure and nothing else.
2. **C2** — the unlit sky renders **exactly** `#87CEEB`. The background is not
   lit, so this is an equality, and it is the falsification test for the whole
   pipeline: if it fails, something is reshaping colour and nothing measured
   afterwards means anything. It runs first.
3. **C3** — the ground renders as its specified albedo `#3D8C40`, hue and
   lightness together, compared in linear space, within a tolerance **computed**
   from the document's own hemisphere ratio and sky colour.
4. **C4** — the scale is the value **minimising** C3's deviation, subject to C1
   and C2.

Reproduce with `tools/find_light_scale.py`; measure one image with
`tools/measure_exposure.py <capture>`. Both print every sample region they read.

## Verdict — light scale 0.2809

| | ground renders | deviation | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Face value (1.0) | `[115, 255, 131]` | 2.8334 | 59.75% of the frame saturated |
| **Settled (0.2809)** | **`[56, 141, 65]`** | **0.0309** | tolerance 0.0401 |
| Specified albedo | `[61, 140, 64]` | — | `#3D8C40` |

Scales 5% and 10% either side both deviate more, so this is a minimum rather than
a stopping point. The sky measured exactly `[135, 206, 235]` at every scale
tested, which is what makes the rest of the numbers trustworthy.

## The rule that was wrong, kept as evidence

The approved design chose the scale as **"the largest value that clips nothing"**.
Applied to this scene it gives **0.6882** — the largest scale at which no pixel of
the frame saturates, with the smallest clipping scale at 0.6910. That produced
`2026-08-29-m2-world-largest-no-clip.png`: grass at `[94, 218, 108]` against a
specified `[61, 140, 64]`, a deviation of **1.6756** against a tolerance of 0.0401.
Forty-two times outside it, and it satisfied every criterion as written.

The hole was that the ground-colour criterion constrained **hue but not
lightness**, so nothing pulled brightness down and "largest" drove the scale up
until saturation stopped it. A second defect compounded it: hue was computed in
gamma-encoded sRGB, where it is not scale-invariant.

That capture is committed deliberately. The criteria were fixed before the images
were judged, exactly as required — and were still wrong, which is worth a picture.

Reproduce the counterexample with
`LPC_LIGHT_SCALE=0.6882 tools/capture.sh res://scenes/world.tscn <out>.png 30`.

## Two other things this change had to find out

**The hemisphere term cannot come from Godot's sky-sourced ambient** (design
D2a). Setting `ambient_light_source = SKY` makes Godot draw the sky and ignore
`background_mode = BG_COLOR`, putting a band of the hemisphere's *ground* colour
across the horizon where §5 requires a flat sky —
`2026-08-29-m2-sky-ambient-rejected.png`, whose row-230 median is
`[32, 139, 32]` against `#228B22`'s `[34, 139, 34]`. The renderer spike's approximation is used
instead: two non-shadowing directional lights, one aimed down carrying the sky
colour and one aimed up carrying the ground colour, **each at the full specified
hemisphere intensity** — a hemisphere light gives an up-facing surface all of its
intensity, so there is nothing to halve. `tests/world_test.gd` guards both, and
`tools/check_sky_ambient.sh` regenerates the rejected capture.

**The ground's edge is visible from the boundary, and the design document
forbids that** — `2026-08-29-m2-boundary.png`, taken from x = +90, the boundary
the scenario names, at roughly the chase camera's eye height and looking outward.

*Keeping the boundary invisible* requires "no wall, fence, or edge of the ground
is drawn or visible" — design document line 460, normative. The same scenario
gives the plane as ±100 wu, the drivable extent is ±90, and fog starts at 50 wu,
which puts the edge five times nearer than fog reaches. **This change does not
satisfy that scenario**, and says so rather than editing the requirement down to
what was built. Recorded as **ambiguity A11**, owned by M6, tracked as `att` 15.

Worth looking at the capture before deciding how bad it is: from that eye height
the edge presents as a horizon line, not as an obvious edge. An earlier version of
this capture was taken from an elevated oblique viewpoint 28 wu inside the limit,
where the same geometry reads unmistakably as a cut-off plane. That framing was
more informative and was not the view the scenario describes, so it was replaced.

## What these captures do not show

No kart, no props, no motion, no HUD — none of those exist yet. The camera is a
**placeholder** at a written-down transform, not the specified chase camera, and
its field of view is Godot's default rather than the document's `fovBase`, so that
a placeholder cannot quietly become the specification.

Regenerate any of these with:

```sh
tools/capture.sh res://scenes/world.tscn docs/progress/2026-08-29-m2-world-settled.png 30
tools/capture.sh res://scenes/boundary_view.tscn docs/progress/2026-08-29-m2-boundary.png 30
LPC_LIGHT_SCALE=1.0 tools/capture.sh res://scenes/world.tscn <out>.png 30
```
