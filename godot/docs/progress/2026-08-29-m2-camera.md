# M2 — the chase camera, and A10

**Status: settled. The camera eases per tick. Settling measured at 0.450 s against
a predicted 0.460 s.**

The last change of M2. It implements the *Chase Camera* feature's first three
scenarios, closes acceptance item 8, and settles **A10** — whether the camera eases
per tick or per frame.

## The thresholds were fixed first, and the ordering is checkable

`docs/camera_thresholds.md` was written and committed in `871541f`, before any
measurement was taken. That commit also carries `chase_camera.gd` and the tuning
fields it reads — it is not thresholds alone, and an earlier version of this
sentence said it was, in a document whose whole argument is about commit ordering.
What matters is unchanged and checkable: `tests/camera_test.gd` did not exist at
`871541f`, and `git diff 871541f HEAD -- godot/docs/camera_thresholds.md` touches
nothing but the appended *Measured* table. That is not a claim in prose: the
commit order is in the history, and the measurements were appended afterwards.

The design document says the camera settles "within roughly half a second", and
"roughly" is an invitation to choose a tolerance after seeing the answer. Every
threshold is derived from the document's own `chaseSmoothing = 0.08` instead.

| quantity | predicted, before measuring | measured |
|---|---|---|
| Time constant | 0.1999 s | **0.200 s** |
| Settling to 10% of peak | 0.460 s | **0.450 s** (limit 0.500) |
| Peak offset through the turn | — | **24.67°** (floor 5°) |
| Field of view at steady state | 89.4° | **89.40°** |

The settling prediction landed within 0.01 s of the measurement, from the easing
factor alone.

## A10: eased per tick

The document states the easing **per tick** and the camera update **once per
frame**. Those are the same sentence at 60 fps and different behaviour everywhere
else: per frame, the time constant becomes 0.4 s at 30 fps and 0.083 s at 144 fps
against a stated 0.2 s.

Resolved as **per tick**. The frame-ordering scenario's *sequence* is normative —
physics, then camera — and its *cardinality* is incidental. What that gives up is
stated in A10: at display rates below the tick rate the camera updates more than
once per frame, which the ordering scenario read literally does not describe.

Two checks carry it. `camera_test.gd` drives the same manoeuvre with ticks grouped
as 1, 2, 3, [0,1,1] and [0,0,1,2] per frame and requires identical camera state —
a regression guard, since stepping per tick makes it true by construction.
`driver_test.gd` carries the half that guard cannot: the composition root really
does advance the camera exactly once per simulation step.

## A11, reported and not decided

A11 asks whether the ground plane's visible edge can be reconciled with the design
document's requirement that no edge be drawn. Earlier evidence came from a
placeholder viewpoint: from an elevated oblique angle the edge reads unmistakably
as an edge, and from a kart-height eye it reads as a horizon.

**Through the camera the document actually specifies**, at the boundary, the
grass-to-sky transition is a single clean line with no wall, fence or discontinuity
visible. That is the most favourable evidence so far for accepting the edge — and
it is still M6's decision, on the whole look rather than on one frame. `att` 15.

## Reproducing

```sh
godot -s tools/drive_capture.gd -- --out <path>.png --ticks 200 --hold accelerate
godot -s tools/drive_capture.gd -- --out <path>.png --ticks 240 --hold accelerate,steer_left
godot -s tools/drive_capture.gd -- --out <path>.png --ticks 560 --hold accelerate
tools/capture.sh res://scenes/main.tscn docs/progress/2026-08-29-m2-camera-fov-rest.png 30
godot --headless -s tests/camera_test.gd     # prints the settling time every run
```

The capture tool prints the kart's final position, because a capture whose subject
sat still looks much like one whose subject drove.
